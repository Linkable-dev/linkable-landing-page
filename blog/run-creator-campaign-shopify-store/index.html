How to run a creator campaign on a Shopify store

A first-timer's walkthrough of running a creator campaign on a Shopify store: the product, the offer, recruiting, shipping, approving content and paying out.

You've got a Shopify store, a product you believe in, and a feeling that creator content would sell it better than another paid ad set. The part nobody explains clearly is what actually happens between deciding to do this and the first piece of content landing in your inbox.

How do you run a creator campaign on a Shopify store?

You run a creator campaign on a Shopify store by picking one product, deciding whether you're paying creators with free product, a fee, or a commission on sales, recruiting a small group of creators, shipping to the ones you approve, reviewing their content, and tracking the sales and engagement that come back through unique links or discount codes. Each of those steps has a failure mode. Most first campaigns go wrong because a brand skips one of them, usually the offer or the tracking.

Platforms built for this, including Linkable, connect to your Shopify catalogue and orders, so the product, the application, the shipment and the sales data sit in one place instead of across email threads and a spreadsheet. If you want the shortest path from zero to a live campaign, our guide to launching your first creator campaign walks through the setup screens. This article is about the decisions behind those screens.

How do you pick the right product and offer?

Choose one product, not your whole catalogue. A hero SKU that's easy to explain on camera, has stock to spare, and doesn't need a long ingredient list to make sense. Trying to seed ten products in one campaign confuses creators about what to post and confuses you about what worked.

Then decide the offer. Gifted product costs you only the unit and shipping and suits creators who'll post for a product they genuinely want. A paid collaboration guarantees content from someone with an established audience, at a fixed fee. Affiliate commission pays only when a tracked sale happens, which protects margin but asks more patience from the creator. Plenty of campaigns mix all three: gifted for a wide first wave, paid for a handful of proven creators, affiliate layered on top of both so everyone has a reason to keep posting. We've set out the trade-offs in more detail in gifted vs paid creator collaborations.

Say you run a skincare brand with a serum that's been your best seller for a few months. A sensible first offer is gifted product plus an affiliate commission on tracked sales, with a paid fee reserved for the two or three creators whose past content you already rate highly.

How do you recruit creators for the campaign?

You can open applications and let creators come to you, or go out and invite people whose content already fits your brand. Most first-timers underuse applications because they assume nobody will apply to an unknown store. In practice, once a campaign is live and visible to creators on a matching platform, applications tend to start arriving within a day, which gives you a pool to choose from instead of a cold list you have to chase. On Linkable, campaigns are matched to creators automatically and the first applications typically come in under 24 hours.

Review applications for fit rather than follower count. Look at whether their existing content shows real use of similar products, how they talk on camera, and whether their audience looks like your customer. We cover the judgement calls in how to evaluate a creator before working with them. If applications alone don't get you enough of the right people, invite creators directly, which is the approach we compare in creator applications vs outbound recruiting.

How do you ship product and approve content?

Once you approve a creator, the order needs to go out fast, with the right variant and a brief that tells them what the content needs to show. A Shopify-connected tool creates the gifted order automatically on approval, pulling from your synced product catalogue so nobody is manually typing addresses into a spreadsheet. A clear brief up front, covering the shot you need, the claims you can and can't make, and the deadline, saves you from asking for a reshoot two weeks later. Our creator brief template for ecommerce is built for exactly that handoff.

Approve content against the brief, not against your personal taste. If the product is shown correctly, the claims are accurate and the shot is usable, approve it even if it isn't the style you'd have chosen yourself. Creators who feel micromanaged stop applying to your next campaign.

How do you track sales and pay creators?

Give each creator their own tracked link or discount code so sales can be attributed to them specifically, rather than lumped into general traffic. Affiliate sales tracked this way let you see which creators are actually driving orders, not just which ones posted the nicest video. Commission payouts then run through the platform rather than through manual invoicing, which matters once you're working with more than a couple of people at a time.

Reporting should show you content delivered, audience reached, engagement generated and sales tracked, both per campaign and per creator, so you can see at a glance who to invite back. This week, pick the one product you'd put in front of a creator without hesitation, decide whether you're offering gifted product, a fee, commission or a mix, and open the campaign for applications. The rest of the process sorts itself out once real creators start replying.

Frequently asked questions

How many creators should a first campaign include?

Start small enough that you can personally review every application and every piece of content, then expand once you know what a good creator looks like for your brand. A tight first batch teaches you more than a large, unmanaged one.

Do I need a written contract for a gifted product campaign?

Yes, even a short one. It should cover what content is expected, by when, and what you can do with it afterwards. See creator contract essentials for small brands for what to include.

Can I run gifted, paid and affiliate offers in the same campaign?

Yes, and many brands do, using gifted product for a wide first wave, paid fees for a few proven creators, and affiliate commission layered on top so everyone has an ongoing reason to post.

What happens if a creator never posts after receiving product?

It happens, which is why the offer and brief should set a clear deadline before you ship. Track who follows through and prioritise re-inviting those creators for future campaigns over chasing no-shows.
