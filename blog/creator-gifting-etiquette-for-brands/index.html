Creator gifting etiquette for brands that creators respect

Gifted product only works when it feels like an offer, not a demand. Here's what respectful creator gifting actually looks like, and where most brands get it wrong.

You send product to ten creators. Two never reply. One posts something flat and generic. Another writes back asking if you also run paid collaborations, as if the free box wasn't quite enough to earn a real post. None of this means your product is wrong. It usually means the gifting itself didn't feel like a partnership.

What creator gifting etiquette for brands looks like in practice

Good creator gifting etiquette for brands means treating a free product as a genuine offer rather than a quiet obligation. You pick the creator because their existing content actually fits what you sell, you say plainly what you're hoping for and what's optional, you leave room for them to decline, and you follow up like a person rather than a reminder system. Creators who sense structure and respect behind the gift are far more likely to post, even with no fee attached.

Say you run a coffee subscription brand and send bags to a handful of creators who already film slow mornings and home routines. If the message explains why you picked them, what the product is, and that posting is appreciated but not required, most will try it and some will post. If the message just says "we'd love to see this on your page," you've turned a gift into an unpaid brief, and plenty of creators will quietly skip it.

Why do creators decline gifted product?

Mostly because the offer feels like work disguised as a present. A vague brief, a product that doesn't match their usual content, or a brand that's clearly mass-sending the same message to a long list all signal the same thing: this isn't really about them. Creators who've been gifted product before and then chased for content learn to treat gifting requests with suspicion, which is fair.

Declining is also just a normal business decision. A creator with a full content calendar might not have room for an unpaid post this month, no matter how much they like the product. Treating a decline as rejection, rather than as information, is where a lot of brands go wrong. Our guide on evaluating a creator before working with them covers how to pick people who are actually likely to say yes.

How do you make gifted product feel like a partnership?

Start with a brief that's honest about the ask. Say what the product is, why you picked them specifically, roughly what kind of content you'd love to see, and that you understand if it's not the right fit right now. Give them something beyond the box too: early access to a new launch, a thank you they can actually use, or a clear path to paid work if the first post lands well.

Gifting works best as the first step of a relationship, not a one-off transaction. Our product seeding campaign strategy post goes into how to structure that first send so it actually gets posts. And if a creator's gifted content performs, tell them, and ask if they'd want to do a paid or affiliate collaboration next. Our gifted vs paid creator collaborations piece explains when to make that move.

What should you never do when gifting to creators?

Don't send product with language that implies a post is guaranteed in exchange for a box worth a fraction of what a paid post would cost. Don't message again two days later asking why they haven't shared anything. Don't treat silence as rudeness. Most creators are juggling several brand relationships at once, and a slow reply usually has nothing to do with your product.

Copy-paste outreach is the other common mistake. If a creator can tell within one line that the same message went to fifty other people, the gift stops feeling personal, and so does the relationship. Our creator outreach message post has examples of messages that read like they were written for one specific person, because they were.

How does gifting fit with paid and affiliate offers?

Gifting, paid fees and affiliate commission aren't separate strategies, they're stages of the same relationship. On Linkable, brands can run gifted product, paid collaboration or affiliate campaigns from the same dashboard, so moving a creator from a free box to a paid post or an affiliate link doesn't mean starting over in a new tool. Creators can see on the creators page exactly how payouts and reporting work before they even apply.

If gifting keeps producing creators who post but never convert into anything bigger, that's usually a sign the follow-up is missing, not that gifting doesn't work. Treat every gifted post as an audition for a longer, paid relationship, and ask the question directly instead of waiting for the creator to raise it.

This week, look at your last round of gifted sends and pick the three creators whose content actually fit your brand. Write each of them a short, specific message asking if they'd want to turn it into a paid or affiliate collaboration. That's the etiquette that actually moves a relationship forward.

Frequently asked questions

Do gifted product collaborations need a contract?

A short agreement is still worth having, even for an unpaid send. It should cover usage rights for the content, whether you can repost it, and any expectations around disclosure, so nothing is assumed on either side.

Should you ask a creator to confirm they'll post before sending product?

Asking for a soft yes before shipping is reasonable, but asking for a guarantee turns a gift into an unpaid brief. It's better to ask about interest and fit, then let the content be their choice once the product arrives.

What if a creator takes the product and never posts?

It happens, and chasing them rarely helps. Note it, don't gift to them again without a paid offer attached, and focus your energy on the creators who did follow through.

How soon should you follow up after sending gifted product?

Give it a reasonable gap before any check-in, and keep the message friendly rather than a reminder about content. A single warm follow-up is enough; repeated pings read as pressure, not partnership.
