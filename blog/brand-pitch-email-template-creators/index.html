Brand pitch email template for creators that works

A short, specific template for pitching brands, plus the three things that make most creator pitches end up ignored: length, flattery and no real idea.

Say you've found a brand you actually want to work with. You've opened a blank email, written a few lines, deleted them, and written a few more. It probably sounds like every other pitch sitting in that brand's inbox, because most creators are working from the same handful of templates they found online, and the person reading it can spot one in the first sentence.

A good brand pitch email template for creators isn't about sounding polished. It's about giving the person reading it a reason to say yes before they've finished scrolling past it on their phone.

What should a brand pitch email template for creators include?

Six things, in this order: who you are, a link to your content, why this brand specifically, one concrete idea, what you're asking for, and a clear next step. Nothing else needs to be in there.

  • A one-line introduction: your name, what you post about, and where.
  • A link to your account, not a paragraph describing it.
  • One sentence on why this brand, tied to something real on their page or in their products.
  • One concrete content idea, described in a sentence or two.
  • What you're asking for: free product, a fee, commission, or a mix.
  • A clear next step, such as the date you'll follow up if you don't hear back.

Each line is doing a job. The introduction tells them whether you're relevant before they click anything. The link lets them check your work without asking. The idea is the part most creators skip, and it's the part that actually gets a reply. We've written about the full structure of a pitch in how to pitch a brand for a collaboration that gets a reply, this piece is about the three ways that structure usually goes wrong.

Why do long pitch emails get ignored?

Because the person opening it is reading on a phone, between other tasks, and a pitch with three paragraphs of your story doesn't get read, it gets skipped. Nobody needs to know how you started posting or why you love the category before they know who you are and what you want.

Keep the whole email short enough to read in one glance. If you can say it in six lines, saying it in sixteen only makes it more likely to be left for later, and later rarely comes.

Why doesn't flattery get you anywhere?

"I love your products" or "I'm such a big fan of your brand" tells the reader nothing, because every pitch they get opens the same way. It doesn't prove you've looked at their page, used their product, or thought about what you'd actually make for them.

Replace it with something specific: a product you've used, a post of theirs that worked, a gap in what they're currently posting. That one sentence does more work than three sentences of praise, and it's the difference between a pitch that reads as personal and one that reads as copied.

What makes an idea concrete enough to pitch?

An idea is concrete when the brand can picture the finished content from your sentence. "I'd love to collaborate" isn't an idea, it's a hope. "A short night routine video using your serum, filmed for people with sensitive skin" is an idea, because it names the format, the product and the angle.

Say you post skincare routines to a few thousand followers. Instead of asking a brand to collaborate in general, you could propose a two-part series: a before video testing their product against your usual routine, and a follow-up a week later showing how your skin reacted. That's specific enough to approve without a call.

What should you do after you hit send?

Most pitches get no reply at all, and that's normal, not a sign your pitch was bad. Follow up once, on the date you said you would in your first email, and if that gets nothing, move on to the next brand rather than sending a third message.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pitch a brand that isn't actively looking for creators?

Yes. Most brands worth working with aren't running an open call, and a specific, well-targeted pitch to someone who runs their creator partnerships often does better than applying to a crowded public campaign.

Should I attach a media kit to my pitch?

A media kit is a short document showing your audience, past content and rates, and it's worth attaching if you have one, but don't delay sending a pitch while you build one. A link to your profile does the same job when you're starting out.

Can I send the same pitch to several brands?

You can reuse the structure, but the line about why this brand specifically has to change each time, otherwise it reads as copied and loses the one thing that makes it worth a reply.

What if I don't hear back after following up once?

Move on. A second or third follow-up rarely changes the outcome, and your time is better spent pitching a new brand than chasing one that's gone quiet.
