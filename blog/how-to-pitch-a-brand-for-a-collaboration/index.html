How to Pitch a Brand for a Collaboration That Gets a Reply

The whole pitch in five moves: pick a brand that fits your content, find the right person, lead with one idea, say what you want, and follow up once.

You've found a brand whose products already show up in your content, and you want to turn that into a collaboration. You open a blank message, stare at it, and write something that sounds like a cover letter. It gets ignored, because brands get a lot of those.

How do you pitch a brand for a collaboration?

Pick a brand whose products already fit what you post, find the person who actually runs creator partnerships rather than a general inbox, lead with one specific content idea instead of a list of your stats, say plainly what you want in return, and follow up once if you hear nothing back. That's the whole pitch. Everything else below is how to do each part well.

Pick a brand that actually fits your content

Start with what you already post, not with what pays the most. A brand whose products contradict your usual content reads as an obvious paid plug, and the person reading your pitch notices that before anyone else does.

Say you post skincare routines to a few thousand followers, mostly reviewing cheap drugstore products. A pitch to a luxury serum brand is a stretch for your audience. A pitch to a brand making affordable exfoliants or sunscreen fits what your followers already expect from you, and the brand can picture your content working for them before they've watched a single video.

If you're not sure where to start, Pitch suggests brands that fit your niche and audience, and puts the ones actively looking for creators at the top, which gives you a shortlist instead of a guess.

Find the person who handles partnerships, not a general inbox

A message to hello@ or a customer service form sits in a queue with returns questions and supplier invoices. Look for whoever runs marketing, partnerships or social for the brand, check their bio and recent posts, and send it to them directly where you can.

This is the part creators skip most often, and it's the part that decides whether anyone reads your pitch at all. If you use Pitch, Linkable looks for the person who runs creator partnerships at the brand, sends your pitch to them, and sends the follow up for you, so you don't have to work out who that is yourself.

Lead with one content idea, not a resume

Don't open with follower counts, engagement claims or a paragraph about how you got into content (we won't say that word again, but you get the idea). Open with one idea for one piece of content: the hook, the format, why it fits their pro

Instead of listing your audience size, you might write: a morning routine video that opens on your skin before sunscreen, shows the product going on, and ends with your actual texture under makeup an hour later. That's one idea, specific enough that the brand can picture the finished video, which does more work than any stat you could give them.

Say what you want and follow up once

Be direct about what you're asking for: free product (sometimes called gifted work), a fixed fee, a commission on sales you drive, or a mix. If you're asking for a fee, work out your own number from the deliverables, the time the shoot and edit will take, whether the brand wants usage rights (permission to reuse your content in their own ads or posts), and whether they want you to avoid working with competitors for a period, which is worth more than a one-off post.

Be honest with yourself about the odds too. Most pitches get no reply, and gifted work is normal while you're building a track record with brands. Send one polite follow up if you hear nothing after a reasonable stretch, then move on to the next brand on your list rather than chasing the same one twice.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a large following to pitch a brand?

No. Brands look for an audience that fits their product, not only a large one, so a smaller, engaged following built around a clear niche can work well.

Can I pitch a brand that isn't already working with creators?

Yes. You can pitch any brand, whether or not they run creator campaigns already. Some brands just haven't set up a process for it yet, and a clear pitch can be what prompts them to start.

Should I ask for payment or free product in my first pitch?

Say clearly what you'd accept rather than leaving it open. Many creators start with gifted product to build a relationship with a brand, then ask for a fee or commission once they've delivered content that performed well.

How long should I wait before following up?

Give the brand a reasonable stretch to reply before sending one short follow up. If there's still no response after that, it's usually better to move on to another brand than to send a second follow up.
