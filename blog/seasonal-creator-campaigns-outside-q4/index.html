Seasonal creator campaigns outside of Q4 cost less

Everyone fights for the same creators in November. Seasonal creator campaigns outside of Q4, from January resets to back to school, cost less and get more attention from both creators and shoppers.

If you run a Shopify store, November is when your inbox fills with creator pitches, your discount codes start multiplying, and every brand in your category is trying to book the same handful of people. By the time Black Friday lands, rates have climbed and the creators worth working with are already full.

Seasonal creator campaigns outside of Q4 are the quieter way around that scramble, and most brands never bother running them. That's a mistake, because the windows either side of the holiday rush are cheaper, less crowded, and often convert just as well for the right category.

Do seasonal creator campaigns outside of Q4 actually work?

Yes. They work because the demand curve that pushes up Q4 prices and fills creators' calendars simply isn't there in January, the spring shoulder months or back to school. You can reach the same creators, usually at a lower fixed fee or a more generous affiliate split, and you're talking to shoppers who are actually looking for something new rather than scrolling past a hundred gift guides a day.

The results don't need to outsell Black Friday to be worth running. A January campaign that costs less to produce and faces far less competition for attention can land a better return on the money you actually spend, even with a smaller top line.

Why does everyone pile into Q4 anyway?

Because Q4 is where the sales are, and nobody wants to be the brand that sat it out. That logic is sound as far as it goes, but it ignores the fact that everyone else is reasoning the same way. The result is a short, expensive window where brands bid against each other for the same creators, and a long, quiet rest of the year where almost nobody is running anything.

If you already have a Q4 plan locked in, our Q4 creator campaign timeline covers how to run that properly. The point here isn't to skip Q4. It's to stop treating it as the only quarter that matters.

What windows outside Q4 are worth running campaigns in?

January is the obvious one. Skincare, supplements and activewear brands all benefit from the reset mindset that follows the holidays, and creators are posting resolution content anyway, so a brand tie-in fits naturally rather than feeling forced.

Back to school works for home, lifestyle, baby and kids brands, and it arrives early enough in the year that you're not competing with the Q4 noise at all. Spring brings its own version for outdoor, travel and fashion brands as people start planning for warmer months. None of these need a holiday theme or a discount code blast. They need a creator talking about a real change in their routine.

How is creator supply different outside Q4?

Outside Q4, creators have more time per brief and fewer competing asks in their inbox. That usually means better content, because they're not rushing between five collaborations in the same week. It also means more room to negotiate: a creator who'd only take a fixed fee in November might be open to a gifted product plus affiliate commission in February, simply because the opportunity cost of their time is lower.

Say you run a skincare brand doing a few hundred orders a month. In November you'd be fighting for slots with every other beauty brand chasing the same list. Run that same outreach in January instead, framed around a reset routine, and you'll likely get faster replies, lower asks, and creators who are genuinely interested in the product rather than slotting you into a gift guide.

How do you brief a campaign for a non-Q4 window?

Tie the brief to the occasion, not the calendar date. A January brief should talk about the habit the creator is changing (clearer skin, a new workout, better sleep), not just say "new year, new you" and leave it there. A back to school brief should talk about the shift in the creator's actual life, whether that's a new routine, a tidier space or packing for a new term.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should you plan a January creator campaign?

Start outreach in early December so creators have the brief before the holidays get busy. Content can still be shot and posted in the first weeks of January without rushing.

Should off-season campaigns use gifting or paid collaborations?

Gifted product tends to work well outside Q4 because creators have more time to engage properly with the product. Mixing in affiliate commission gives them an incentive to keep posting beyond the first video.

Does affiliate commission perform differently outside Q4?

Tracked sales outside Q4 are usually smaller in volume but easier to attribute, since there isn't a flood of competing discount codes and ads in the same week confusing the picture.

Which brand categories benefit most from non-Q4 campaigns?

Skincare, supplements and activewear suit January resets. Home, lifestyle, baby and kids brands suit back to school. Outdoor and travel brands suit the spring shoulder months.
